01 The problem

A 12-word phrase is all your money written on a piece of paper.

Almost every crypto wallet asks you to copy it by hand. And the day you need it is often the day it's too late.

You lose it

A move, a fire, a sheet thrown away by mistake: your funds are gone forever. No one can recover them for you.

Someone finds it

A photo on your phone, a notebook in a drawer: anyone who reads it can empty your wallet in minutes.

You retype it

On a fake site or an infected computer, typing it is enough to hand it to thieves. It's the most common scam.

With OpenPGP Wallet, that phrase doesn't exist. You never see it, never write it down, never type it: there's nothing to lose and nothing to steal.

02 The backup

Three pieces. Any two are enough. One alone is useless.

When you create your wallet, the app splits its key into three pieces, like a treasure map torn in three. Any two pieces rebuild everything; a single piece reveals nothing, even to an expert.

  • Keep them on USB drives: one at home, one with someone close, one at the office. Lose one, no harm done.
  • Each piece is sealed in the OpenPGP format, the standard that has encrypted e-mail for over 30 years: the slightest damage is detected.
  • Several wallets can share the same USB drives, each stored under its own name.
  • The app checks your backup drives whenever you like, and reminds you once a year.

07 What's next · FROST

Tomorrow, the signature will be split too.

Today, the backup is split into three pieces. With FROST, every send will be signed jointly: your computer and your phone each produce their share of the signature, without ever bringing the key together. Malware on the computer will no longer be enough to move your funds.

  • The whole key no longer exists anywhere, neither on the computer nor on the phone, not even while signing.
  • Your phone shows the recipient and the amount, and you approve at a glance: a Ledger's trusted screen, with the device already in your pocket.
  • A public protocol, standardized by the IETF (RFC 9591), with an open-source Rust implementation developed by the Zcash Foundation: we'll reuse it rather than reinvent cryptography.
  • Planned first for shielded Zcash, Solana, NEAR, Stellar and Bitcoin (Taproot addresses).

10 FAQ

Real questions, clear answers.

What if I lose my computer?

Nothing is lost. Install the app on another computer and plug in two of your three backup drives: your wallet comes back exactly as it was, and the app shows you your addresses to check before you confirm.

What if someone steals a USB drive?

A single piece can't do anything: it takes two to rebuild the wallet. Keep the other two safe. As a precaution, you can also move your funds to a new wallet.

I forgot my password. Is it over?

No. Restore your wallet with two of your backup drives and choose a new password. Your funds are only lost if you lose both your password and two of your three backups.

Who holds my funds?

Only you. The app is "non-custodial": no account, no server, nothing kept on our side. No one can shut down your wallet (only some token issuers, such as those of USDC or tokenized stocks, can freeze their own tokens)… and no one can recover it for you, which is why your backups matter.

How much does it cost?

The app is free. It only earns a small fee on swaps and staking (0 to 1% depending on the service), always shown before you confirm. Network fees, paid to the blockchains, still apply as everywhere.

Are tokenized stocks real stocks?

They are tokens issued by Backed Assets (xStocks) that track the price of a stock or fund; according to their issuer, they are backed by the corresponding securities. They carry no voting rights. They are not for US persons and are not offered in the UK, Canada or Australia.

What is FROST, in a nutshell?

A way of signing jointly. Today, your key is rebuilt for a brief moment on the computer to sign a send. With FROST, the computer and the phone each sign their share and the whole key never exists: to steal your funds, someone would have to hack both devices at the same time.

Why only Windows?

To get one platform right before opening others. A mobile version is in preparation: it will also be the second device for FROST.

Can I use it now?

Not with real amounts yet: the software hasn't been audited by an independent expert. The public version will come after that audit, code signing and the source code release.
